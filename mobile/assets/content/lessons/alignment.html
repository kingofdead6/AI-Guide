The KL term stops reward hacking: drifting into strange outputs that fool the reward model. PPO (Policy Gradients, Actor–Critic & PPO) is the RL algorithm traditionally used.

This is a simple classification-style loss with no sampling and no separate reward model.

RL with verifiable rewards

For math and code, the reward can be checked automatically (is the answer correct, do the tests pass). Training on such rewards elicits longer step-by-step reasoning. GRPO samples a group of GGG answers per prompt and uses group-normalized advantages Ai=(ri−mean⁡(r))/std⁡(r)A_i=(r_i-\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf r))/\operatorname{std}(\mathbf r)Ai​=(ri​−mean(r))/std(r) instead of a learned value function. Related ideas: RLAIF and constitution-based training, where AI feedback guided by written principles replaces some human labels.

Big picture

SFT shows the model good examples. Preference methods show it comparisons ("this is better than that"), which are easier for humans to give and carry information about what to avoid. RL on verifiable tasks lets the model discover strategies no one demonstrated.
