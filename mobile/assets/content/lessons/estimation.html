Maximum likelihood

Taking the log turns a product of tiny numbers into a sum; that is numerically stable and easy to differentiate.

MAP estimation and regularization

θ^MAP=arg⁡max⁡θ  log⁡p(D∣θ)+log⁡p(θ)\hat\theta_{\text{MAP}}=\arg\max_\theta\;\log p(\mathcal D\mid\theta)+\log p(\theta)θ^MAP​=argθmax​logp(D∣θ)+logp(θ)

With a Gaussian prior θ∼N(0,τ2I)\theta\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2\mathbf I)θ∼N(0,τ2I), −log⁡p(θ)=12τ2∥θ∥22+const-\log p(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\theta\|_2^2+\text{const}−logp(θ)=2τ21​∥θ∥22​+const: L2 regularization (ridge, weight decay). A Laplace prior gives L1 (lasso). Regularization is a prior belief that parameters are small.

Estimators, LLN and CLT

  • An estimator's error decomposes as MSE(θ^)=Bias(θ^)2+Var⁡(θ^)\text{MSE}(\hat\theta)=\text{Bias}(\hat\theta)^2+\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)MSE(θ^)=Bias(θ^)2+Var(θ^).
  • Law of large numbers: Xˉn→μ\bar X_n\to\muXˉn​→μ as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞. This justifies replacing the expected risk with the empirical average.
  • Central limit theorem: n(Xˉn−μ)→N(0,σ2)\sqrt n(\bar X_n-\mu)\to\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)n​(Xˉn​−μ)→N(0,σ2). Hence the 95% confidence interval xˉ±1.96 s/n\bar x\pm1.96\,s/\sqrt nxˉ±1.96s/n​.

Hypothesis testing (A/B tests, model comparison)

State a null hypothesis H0H_0H0​ (no difference), compute a test statistic, and get the p-value: the probability of a result at least this extreme if H0H_0H0​ were true. A small p-value is evidence against H0H_0H0​; it is not the probability H0H_0H0​ is true. For comparing two conversion rates, the two-proportion z-test uses z=(p^1−p^2)/p^(1−p^)(1/n1+1/n2)z=(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)/\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)(1/n_1+1/n_2)}z=(p^​1​−p^​2​)/p^​(1−p^​)(1/n1​+1/n2​)​, where p^\hat pp^​ is the pooled rate.
