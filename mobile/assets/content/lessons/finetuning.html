Why full fine-tuning is expensive

With Adam in mixed precision you store roughly: bf16 weights (2 bytes) + bf16 gradients (2) + fp32 master weights (4) + two fp32 Adam moments (8) ≈ 16 bytes per parameter, before activations. A 7B model needs about 112 GB.

LoRA

Freeze the pretrained W0∈Rd×k\mathbf W_0\in\mathbb R^{d\times k}W0​∈Rd×k and learn a low-rank update:

W=W0+αrBA,B∈Rd×r, A∈Rr×k, r≪min⁡(d,k)\mathbf W=\mathbf W_0+\frac{\alpha}{r}\mathbf B\mathbf A,\qquad \mathbf B\in\mathbb R^{d\times r},\ \mathbf A\in\mathbb R^{r\times k},\ r\ll\min(d,k)W=W0​+rα​BA,B∈Rd×r, A∈Rr×k, r≪min(d,k)

Trainable parameters drop from dkdkdk to r(d+k)r(d+k)r(d+k). B\mathbf BB is initialized to zero, so training starts exactly at the pretrained model. After training, BA\mathbf B\mathbf ABA can be merged into W0\mathbf W_0W0​ for zero inference overhead, or kept separate to swap adapters per task.

Quantization

Map floats to bbb-bit integers with a scale sss and zero point zzz:

q=round⁡ ⁣(xs)+z,x^=s (q−z),s=xmax⁡−xmin⁡2b−1q=\operatorname{round}\!\Big(\frac{x}{s}\Big)+z,\qquad \hat x=s\,(q-z),\qquad s=\frac{x_{\max}-x_{\min}}{2^b-1}q=round(sx​)+z,x^=s(q−z),s=2b−1xmax​−xmin​​

Weight memory for inference: 7B parameters × 2 bytes (fp16) ≈ 14 GB; 8-bit ≈ 7 GB; 4-bit ≈ 3.5 GB (plus overhead). Per-group scales (e.g. every 64–128 weights) and outlier-aware methods (GPTQ, AWQ) keep quality high. QLoRA fine-tunes LoRA adapters on top of a frozen 4-bit base model.

When to fine-tune at all

Try prompting and RAG first. Fine-tune to teach a format, style, or behaviour, to specialize a small model to beat a big one on a narrow task, or to cut prompt length and latency. Use RAG for knowledge that changes or must be cited.

from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, BitsAndBytesConfig
from peft import LoraConfig, get_peft_model
bnb = BitsAndBytesConfig(load_in_4bit=True, bnb_4bit_quant_type="nf4",
                         bnb_4bit_compute_dtype=torch.bfloat16)
base = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("YOUR_BASE_MODEL", quantization_config=bnb)
cfg = LoraConfig(r=16, lora_alpha=32, lora_dropout=0.05, task_type="CAUSAL_LM",
                 target_modules=["q_proj", "k_proj", "v_proj", "o_proj"])
model = get_peft_model(base, cfg)
model.print_trainable_parameters()       # typically well under 1% of the total
# then train with transformers' Trainer or TRL's SFTTrainer on (prompt, response) data
