Predictions average over all plausible parameters. Two kinds of uncertainty: aleatoric (noise in the data, irreducible) and epistemic (lack of data, shrinks with more data).

Gaussian processes

GPs are excellent with small data and calibrated uncertainty, but exact inference costs O(n3)O(n^3)O(n3); sparse/inducing-point approximations scale them up. They are the standard surrogate in Bayesian optimization (next lesson).

Uncertainty for deep networks

Exact posteriors are intractable, so we approximate: variational inference (fit a simple q(θ)q(\theta)q(θ) by maximizing an ELBO, Autoencoders & Variational Autoencoders), Laplace approximations, MCMC for small models, and the practical favourites: deep ensembles (train 5 networks from different seeds and use their disagreement) and MC dropout (keep dropout on at test time and average several passes). Always check calibration.

from sklearn.gaussian_process import GaussianProcessRegressor
from sklearn.gaussian_process.kernels import RBF, WhiteKernel
X = np.array([[0.8], [2.2], [3.5], [5.1], [7.4], [8.6]]); y = np.sin(X).ravel()
gp = GaussianProcessRegressor(RBF(length_scale=1.0) + WhiteKernel(1e-3)).fit(X, y)
xs = np.linspace(0, 10, 200)[:, None]
mean, std = gp.predict(xs, return_std=True)        # wide std far from the data
print(gp.kernel_)                                  # hyperparameters fit by marginal likelihood
