Regularization

  • Weight decay (L2 / AdamW).
  • Dropout: during training zero each unit with probability ppp and scale survivors by 1/(1−p)1/(1-p)1/(1−p) ("inverted dropout") so expectations match at test time. It acts like training an ensemble of subnetworks.
  • Data augmentation (Images, Augmentation & Transfer Learning), early stopping on validation loss, label smoothing (target 1−ε1-\varepsilon1−ε for the true class, spreading ε\varepsilonε over the others).

Stability and speed

  • Gradient clipping: if ∥g∥>c\|\mathbf g\|\gt c∥g∥>c, rescale g←c g/∥g∥\mathbf g\leftarrow c\,\mathbf g/\|\mathbf g\|g←cg/∥g∥. Standard for RNNs and Transformers.
  • Mixed precision: compute in fp16/bf16, keep master weights in fp32; roughly 2× faster and half the memory.
  • Residual connections (Convolutional Neural Networks) and normalization are what make 100+ layer networks trainable.

Debugging recipe that saves days

  1. Check the initial loss: for CCC balanced classes it should be about log⁡C\log ClogC (2.30 for 10 classes).
  2. Overfit one small batch to near-zero loss. If you can't, there's a bug.
  3. Sweep learning rates on a log scale (1e-5 … 1e-1); the right one is usually just below where loss explodes.
  4. Plot train and validation loss, and gradient norms per layer.
  5. Only then add regularization and scale up.
