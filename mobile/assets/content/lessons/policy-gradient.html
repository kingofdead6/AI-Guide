∇θJ=∇θ∫pθ(τ)R(τ) dτ=∫pθ(τ)∇θlog⁡pθ(τ)R(τ) dτ=Eτ[∑t∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st) Gt]\nabla_\theta J=\nabla_\theta\int p_\theta(\tau)R(\tau)\,d\tau=\int p_\theta(\tau)\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)R(\tau)\,d\tau=\mathbb E_\tau\Big[\sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,G_t\Big]∇θ​J=∇θ​∫pθ​(τ)R(τ)dτ=∫pθ​(τ)∇θ​logpθ​(τ)R(τ)dτ=Eτ​[t∑​∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)Gt​]

The environment's transition probabilities don't depend on θ\thetaθ, so they drop out of ∇log⁡pθ(τ)\nabla\log p_\theta(\tau)∇logpθ​(τ): we don't need a model of the world. This is REINFORCE: increase the log-probability of actions in proportion to the return that followed them.

Reducing variance

Subtracting a baseline b(st)b(s_t)b(st​) keeps the gradient unbiased but lowers variance. With b=V(st)b=V(s_t)b=V(st​) we get the advantage At=Gt−V(st)A_t=G_t-V(s_t)At​=Gt​−V(st​): "how much better than expected was this action?".
