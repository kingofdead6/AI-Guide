Convexity

fff is convex if f(λx+(1−λ)y)≤λf(x)+(1−λ)f(y)f(\lambda\mathbf x+(1-\lambda)\mathbf y)\le\lambda f(\mathbf x)+(1-\lambda)f(\mathbf y)f(λx+(1−λ)y)≤λf(x)+(1−λ)f(y), equivalently (if twice differentiable) H⪰0\mathbf H\succeq0H⪰0. Every local minimum of a convex function is global. Linear and logistic regression and SVMs are convex. Neural networks are not, yet gradient methods still work remarkably well on them.

Why the learning rate has a ceiling

On a quadratic L=12θ⊤Hθ\mathcal L=\tfrac12\theta^\top\mathbf H\thetaL=21​θ⊤Hθ, the update is θt+1=(I−ηH)θt\theta_{t+1}=(\mathbf I-\eta\mathbf H)\theta_tθt+1​=(I−ηH)θt​. Along eigenvector iii the error is multiplied by 1−ηλi1-\eta\lambda_i1−ηλi​ each step, so GD converges iff 0<η<2/λmax⁡0\lt\eta\lt2/\lambda_{\max}0<η<2/λmax​. The slowest direction shrinks by 1−ηλmin⁡1-\eta\lambda_{\min}1−ηλmin​. With η≈1/λmax⁡\eta\approx1/\lambda_{\max}η≈1/λmax​ that is 1−1/κ1-1/\kappa1−1/κ: ill-conditioned problems (large κ\kappaκ) are slow. This is why you standardize features and why normalization layers and adaptive optimizers help.

Learning-rate schedules

Warmup increases η\etaη linearly for the first steps (Adam's moment estimates are unreliable early). Cosine decay then lowers it smoothly:

ηt=ηmin⁡+12(ηmax⁡−ηmin⁡)(1+cos⁡πtT)\eta_t=\eta_{\min}+\tfrac12(\eta_{\max}-\eta_{\min})\Big(1+\cos\frac{\pi t}{T}\Big)ηt​=ηmin​+21​(ηmax​−ηmin​)(1+cosTπt​)

Constrained optimization: Lagrange multipliers

To minimize f(x)f(\mathbf x)f(x) subject to g(x)=0g(\mathbf x)=0g(x)=0, solve ∇f=λ∇g\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g∇f=λ∇g: at the optimum the gradients are parallel. Formally, find stationary points of L(x,λ)=f(x)−λg(x)\mathcal L(\mathbf x,\lambda)=f(\mathbf x)-\lambda g(\mathbf x)L(x,λ)=f(x)−λg(x). With inequality constraints this generalizes to the KKT conditions, which give SVM their support vectors (Support Vector Machines & Kernels). PCA is derived this way too (Unsupervised Learning: k-Means, GMMs, EM & PCA).

# GD vs momentum vs Adam on an ill-conditioned quadratic (kappa = 100)
H = np.diag([1.0, 100.0]); grad = lambda th: H @ th
def run(opt, steps=200, lr=0.009):
    th, m, v = np.array([1.0, 1.0]), np.zeros(2), np.zeros(2)
    for t in range(1, steps + 1):
        g = grad(th)
        if opt == "gd":
            th = th - lr * g
        elif opt == "momentum":
            m = 0.9 * m + g; th = th - lr * m
        else:  # adam
            m = 0.9 * m + 0.1 * g; v = 0.999 * v + 0.001 * g**2
            mh, vh = m / (1 - 0.9**t), v / (1 - 0.999**t)
            th = th - 0.05 * mh / (np.sqrt(vh) + 1e-8)
    return 0.5 * th @ H @ th
for o in ["gd", "momentum", "adam"]: print(o, run(o))
