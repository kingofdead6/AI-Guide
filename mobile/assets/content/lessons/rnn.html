Why gradients vanish or explode

∂hT∂ht=∏k=t+1Tdiag⁡(1−hk2) Whh\frac{\partial\mathbf h_T}{\partial\mathbf h_t}=\prod_{k=t+1}^{T}\operatorname{diag}\big(1-\mathbf h_k^2\big)\,\mathbf W_{hh}∂ht​∂hT​​=k=t+1∏T​diag(1−hk2​)Whh​

A product of T−tT-tT−t matrices behaves like WhhT−t\mathbf W_{hh}^{T-t}WhhT−t​ (Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors & SVD): if its largest singular value is below 1 the gradient vanishes exponentially, above 1 it explodes. So plain RNNs can't learn long-range dependencies. Clipping handles explosion; gating handles vanishing.

The cell state is updated additively, so ∂ct/∂ct−1=diag⁡(ft)\partial\mathbf c_t/\partial\mathbf c_{t-1}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf f_t)∂ct​/∂ct−1​=diag(ft​). When the forget gate is near 1, information and gradients flow across many steps: the same trick as residual connections. The GRU merges gates into update and reset gates, with fewer parameters and similar performance.

Sequence-to-sequence and the bottleneck

For translation, an encoder RNN compresses the source into one vector and a decoder RNN generates the target from it. Squeezing a whole sentence into one fixed vector is a bottleneck. Attention (Bahdanau, 2014) let the decoder look back at all encoder states. The Transformer then kept attention and dropped recurrence entirely.

lstm = nn.LSTM(input_size=32, hidden_size=128, num_layers=2, batch_first=True, dropout=0.1)
