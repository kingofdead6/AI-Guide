Pipeline

Normalize (lowercase, Unicode normalization, and for Arabic, normalizing letter variants and removing diacritics where appropriate) → tokenize → optionally remove stopwords and stem or lemmatize → vectorize.

Bag of words and TF-IDF

A document becomes a vector of word counts (word order lost). TF-IDF reweights so words frequent in this document but rare across the corpus count most:

$$\text{tfidf}(t,d)=\text{tf}(t,d)\cdot\log\frac{N}{\text{df}(t)}$$

where \(N\) is the number of documents and \(\text{df}(t)\) the number containing term \(t\). Libraries add smoothing (scikit-learn uses \(\log\frac{1+N}{1+\text{df}}+1\)).

n-gram language models

$$P(w_t\mid w_{t-n+1:t-1})\approx\frac{\text{count}(w_{t-n+1:t})}{\text{count}(w_{t-n+1:t-1})},\qquad \text{PPL}=\exp\Big(-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\log P(w_i\mid\text{context})\Big)$$

Counts are sparse, so smoothing is required (add-one, Kneser–Ney). Neural language models replaced counts with learned representations that generalize to unseen contexts, but perplexity remains the standard intrinsic metric.

Classic tasks

Text classification (sentiment, topic, spam), named-entity recognition, part-of-speech tagging, question answering, summarization, translation. Today most are solved by fine-tuning pretrained Transformers (6.4) or prompting LLMs ([[course:llm]]).
