The multivariate Gaussian

$$\mathcal N(\mathbf{x}\mid\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\Sigma)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\boldsymbol\Sigma|^{1/2}}\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac12(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu)^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu)\Big)$$

Contours are ellipses whose axes are the eigenvectors of \(\boldsymbol\Sigma\), with lengths proportional to \(\sqrt{\lambda_i}\). Affine maps keep it Gaussian: if \(\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal N(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\Sigma)\) then \(\mathbf{Ax}+\mathbf{b}\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol\mu+\mathbf{b},\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{A}^\top)\). Sampling uses exactly this: \(\mathbf{x}=\boldsymbol\mu+\mathbf{L}\boldsymbol\epsilon\) with \(\mathbf{LL}^\top=\boldsymbol\Sigma\), \(\boldsymbol\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbf I)\). Hold onto that: it is the reparameterization trick ([[vae]]).
