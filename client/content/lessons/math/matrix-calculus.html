Derivation: \(\nabla_\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{Ax})\)

$$f=\sum_i\sum_j x_iA_{ij}x_j\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_k}=\sum_j A_{kj}x_j+\sum_i x_iA_{ik}=(\mathbf{Ax})_k+(\mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{x})_k$$

Derivation: the linear layer

With \(y_i=\sum_j W_{ij}x_j\), the chain rule gives \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial W_{ij}=g_i\,x_j\), which is the outer product \(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{x}^\top\). And \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial x_j=\sum_i g_iW_{ij}=(\mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{g})_j\). These two lines are the whole backward pass of a dense layer.

Always check your gradients numerically

def num_grad(f, x, eps=1e-6):
    g = np.zeros_like(x)
    for i in range(x.size):
        e = np.zeros_like(x); e.flat[i] = eps
        g.flat[i] = (f(x + e) - f(x - e)) / (2 * eps)   # central difference
    return g

A = np.random.randn(3, 3); x = np.random.randn(3)
f = lambda v: v @ A @ v
print(np.allclose(num_grad(f, x), (A + A.T) @ x, atol=1e-5))   # True
