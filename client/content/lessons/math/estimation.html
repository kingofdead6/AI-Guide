Maximum likelihood

Given i.i.d. data \(\mathcal D=\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}\) and a model \(p(x\mid\theta)\):

$$\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}}=\arg\max_\theta\prod_{i=1}^np(x_i\mid\theta)=\arg\min_\theta\;-\sum_{i=1}^n\log p(x_i\mid\theta)$$

Taking the log turns a product of tiny numbers into a sum; that is numerically stable and easy to differentiate.

Derivation: Gaussian MLE

$$\ell(\mu,\sigma^2)=-\frac n2\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2$$ $$\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\mu}=\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_i(x_i-\mu)=0\Rightarrow\hat\mu=\bar x,\qquad \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\sigma^2}=0\Rightarrow\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2$$

\(\mathbb E[\hat\sigma^2]=\frac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2\), so the MLE of the variance is biased. Dividing by \(n-1\) (Bessel's correction) fixes it.

The big insight: losses are likelihoods.

Assume the target is…Negative log-likelihood becomes
\(y\sim\mathcal N(f_\theta(\mathbf x),\sigma^2)\)Mean squared error
\(y\sim\text{Laplace}(f_\theta(\mathbf x),b)\)Mean absolute error
\(y\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\sigma(f_\theta(\mathbf x)))\)Binary cross-entropy
\(y\sim\text{Categorical}(\operatorname{softmax}(f_\theta(\mathbf x)))\)Cross-entropy (classifiers, LLMs)

MAP estimation and regularization

$$\hat\theta_{\text{MAP}}=\arg\max_\theta\;\log p(\mathcal D\mid\theta)+\log p(\theta)$$

With a Gaussian prior \(\theta\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2\mathbf I)\), \(-\log p(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\theta\|_2^2+\text{const}\): L2 regularization (ridge, weight decay). A Laplace prior gives L1 (lasso). Regularization is a prior belief that parameters are small.

Estimators, LLN and CLT

  • An estimator's error decomposes as \(\text{MSE}(\hat\theta)=\text{Bias}(\hat\theta)^2+\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)\).
  • Law of large numbers: \(\bar X_n\to\mu\) as \(n\to\infty\). This justifies replacing the expected risk with the empirical average.
  • Central limit theorem: \(\sqrt n(\bar X_n-\mu)\to\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\). Hence the 95% confidence interval \(\bar x\pm1.96\,s/\sqrt n\).

Hypothesis testing (A/B tests, model comparison)

State a null hypothesis \(H_0\) (no difference), compute a test statistic, and get the p-value: the probability of a result at least this extreme if \(H_0\) were true. A small p-value is evidence against \(H_0\); it is not the probability \(H_0\) is true. For comparing two conversion rates, the two-proportion z-test uses \(z=(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)/\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)(1/n_1+1/n_2)}\), where \(\hat p\) is the pooled rate.

from scipy import stats
data = np.random.normal(loc=5.0, scale=2.0, size=500)
mu_hat, sigma_hat = data.mean(), data.std()        # MLE (ddof=0)
ci = stats.t.interval(0.95, df=len(data)-1, loc=mu_hat, scale=stats.sem(data))
t, p = stats.ttest_1samp(data, popmean=5.0)        # H0: mean = 5
Practice problems
  1. Derive the MLE of a Bernoulli parameter from \(k\) successes in \(n\) trials.
    Solution
    \(\ell(p)=k\log p+(n-k)\log(1-p)\). Setting \(\ell'(p)=\frac kp-\frac{n-k}{1-p}=0\) gives \(\hat p=k/n\).
  2. Show that maximizing a Gaussian likelihood with fixed \(\sigma\) is the same as minimizing MSE.
    Solution
    \(-\log p(y_i\mid\mathbf x_i)=\frac{(y_i-f(\mathbf x_i))^2}{2\sigma^2}+\text{const}\). Summing and dropping constants leaves \(\sum_i(y_i-f(\mathbf x_i))^2\).
