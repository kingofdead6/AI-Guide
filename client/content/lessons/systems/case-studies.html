1 · Video / news feed recommendation

Goal: maximize long-term satisfaction (watch time, return visits), not just clicks. Architecture: the multi-stage funnel ([[recsys]]): two-tower and co-visitation candidate generators → multi-task ranking model (click, watch time, like, skip) → re-ranking for diversity, freshness and policy. Data: impressions with outcomes; handle position bias. Serving: pre-computed item embeddings in an ANN index, real-time user features from recent activity. Pitfalls: clickbait optimization, filter bubbles, cold-start creators. Metrics: offline recall@k and NDCG; online watch time, retention, survey satisfaction.

2 · Search ranking

Pipeline: query understanding (spelling, intent, entities) → retrieval (BM25 + dense, [[retrieval-ranking]]) → learning-to-rank with GBDT or cross-encoders over query–document features → personalization. Labels: clicks corrected for position bias, plus human relevance ratings. Losses: pointwise, pairwise (RankNet), listwise (LambdaMART). Metrics: NDCG@10, MRR offline; success rate and reformulation rate online.

3 · Payment fraud detection

Constraints: decision in tens of milliseconds, extreme class imbalance (≪1% fraud), adversaries who adapt, labels delayed by weeks (chargebacks). Model: GBDT on velocity features (transactions per card in the last hour), device and merchant risk, graph features (shared devices between accounts), with rules as a safety net. Decisions: thresholds chosen by expected cost (fraud loss vs blocked good customers), with a manual review band. Metrics: precision at a fixed review capacity, recall, dollar-weighted metrics. Monitoring: drift as fraud patterns change; frequent retraining.

4 · Customer-support assistant (RAG + agent)

Pipeline: ingest help-centre articles and past resolved tickets → hybrid retrieval + reranking → LLM answers with citations → tools for account lookups behind authentication → escalation to a human when confidence is low or the user asks. Safety: no actions on accounts without confirmation, PII redaction in logs, prompt-injection defences. Evaluation: a labelled set of real questions; retrieval recall@k; answer correctness and groundedness by rubric; online resolution rate, escalation rate, CSAT. Cost: route simple FAQs to a small model, cache stable content.

5 · Content moderation

Inputs: text, images, video, multilingual. Architecture: fast cheap classifiers on everything → expensive multimodal models on borderline content → human reviewers for the hardest cases and appeals; reviewer decisions become training data. Trade-offs: precision vs recall differ per policy (much higher recall for the most severe harms). Pitfalls: adversarial evasion (misspellings, text in images), cultural and language context, reviewer wellbeing. Metrics: prevalence of violating content seen by users, precision/recall per policy, time to action.
