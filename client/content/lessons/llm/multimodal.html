Common architectures

  • Encoder + projector + LLM (LLaVA-style): a pretrained vision encoder (often CLIP-like) turns an image into patch embeddings; a small MLP projects them into the LLM's embedding space; they are inserted into the sequence as "visual tokens" next to text tokens.
  • Cross-attention (Flamingo-style): the LLM attends to image features through extra cross-attention layers, keeping the text sequence short.
  • Native multimodal: images, audio and text are all tokenized (e.g. with discrete codebooks, [[flows]]) and one Transformer is trained on the interleaved stream; it can also generate non-text outputs.

Training recipe

  1. Alignment: freeze the encoder and LLM, train only the projector on image–caption pairs.
  2. Visual instruction tuning: unfreeze more and train on (image, question, answer) conversations, documents, charts, OCR-heavy data.
  3. Preference tuning to reduce hallucinated visual details.

Practical notes

Images cost tokens: a 336×336 image with 14×14 patches is 576 tokens, and high-resolution "tiling" multiplies that. Typical failure modes: counting, fine spatial relations, small text, and confidently describing objects that aren't there. Evaluate on your own images; for documents, compare against OCR ground truth.
