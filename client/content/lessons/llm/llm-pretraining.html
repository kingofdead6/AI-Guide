The objective

$$p_\theta(x_1,\dots,x_T)=\prod_{t=1}^Tp_\theta(x_t\mid x_{\lt t}),\qquad \mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{\lt t})$$

This is the chain rule of probability ([[variance-joint]]) plus maximum likelihood ([[estimation]]). Thanks to the causal mask, one forward pass over a sequence of \(T\) tokens trains on \(T\) predictions at once.

The pipeline

  1. Data: web crawl, books, code, papers; deduplication, quality filtering, toxicity and PII filtering, language and domain mixing. Data quality is one of the biggest levers.
  2. Pretraining: next-token prediction over trillions of tokens. Produces a base model that continues text but doesn't follow instructions reliably.
  3. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT): train on curated (instruction, response) pairs.
  4. Preference tuning / RL: RLHF, DPO, or RL with verifiable rewards ([[alignment]]) to make responses helpful, honest and safe, and to improve reasoning.

Compute and scaling laws

Training compute for a dense model is about \(C\approx6ND\) FLOPs (\(N\) parameters, \(D\) tokens): about \(2N\) per token forward and \(4N\) backward. Empirically, loss falls as a power law in model size, data and compute:

$$\mathcal L(N,D)\approx E+\frac{A}{N^\alpha}+\frac{B}{D^\beta}$$

The "Chinchilla" analysis (2022) found that for a fixed compute budget, parameters and tokens should grow together, roughly 20 tokens per parameter. Many recent models train far beyond that ratio because smaller, over-trained models are cheaper to serve.

Modern architecture choices

Typical recent decoder LLMs use pre-norm with RMSNorm, RoPE positions, SwiGLU feed-forward layers (\(\operatorname{FFN}(\mathbf x)=\mathbf W_2(\operatorname{SiLU}(\mathbf W_1\mathbf x)\odot\mathbf W_3\mathbf x)\)), grouped-query attention, no biases, and often mixture-of-experts layers ([[inference]]). Exact recipes differ between labs and change quickly.
