The policy gradient theorem

Objective \(J(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]\) over trajectories \(\tau\). Using the log-derivative trick \(\nabla p=p\,\nabla\log p\):

$$\nabla_\theta J=\nabla_\theta\int p_\theta(\tau)R(\tau)\,d\tau=\int p_\theta(\tau)\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)R(\tau)\,d\tau=\mathbb E_\tau\Big[\sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,G_t\Big]$$

The environment's transition probabilities don't depend on \(\theta\), so they drop out of \(\nabla\log p_\theta(\tau)\): we don't need a model of the world. This is REINFORCE: increase the log-probability of actions in proportion to the return that followed them.

Reducing variance

Subtracting a baseline \(b(s_t)\) keeps the gradient unbiased but lowers variance. With \(b=V(s_t)\) we get the advantage \(A_t=G_t-V(s_t)\): "how much better than expected was this action?". Actor–critic methods learn the policy (actor) and \(V\) (critic) together.

PPO

Large policy updates are destructive, so PPO clips the probability ratio \(r_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)/\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t\mid s_t)\):

$$\mathcal L^{\text{CLIP}}=\mathbb E_t\Big[\min\big(r_t(\theta)A_t,\ \operatorname{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t\big)\Big]$$

In RLHF the "environment" is a prompt, the "action" is the generated response (token by token), and the reward comes from a reward model or a verifier, minus the KL penalty from [[alignment]].
