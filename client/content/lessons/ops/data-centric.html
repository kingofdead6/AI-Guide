Labelling

  • Write labelling guidelines with definitions, edge cases and examples; revise them as disagreements surface.
  • Measure agreement between annotators with Cohen's κ, which corrects for chance: $$\kappa=\frac{p_o-p_e}{1-p_e}$$ (\(p_o\) observed agreement, \(p_e\) agreement expected by chance). Low κ means the task or guidelines are ambiguous, and no model will beat that ceiling.
  • Have several people label a "gold" set used for evaluation, and spot-check production labels.

Finding and fixing bad data

Label noise is common (often a few percent even in benchmark datasets). Find suspicious examples by training with cross-validation and flagging items where the out-of-fold prediction confidently disagrees with the label (the idea behind confident learning). Also: deduplicate (exact and near-duplicate), remove leakage between splits, filter low-quality text, strip personal data, and check class balance per segment.

Getting more signal per label

  • Active learning: label the examples the model is least sure about (highest entropy, smallest margin) instead of random ones.
  • Weak supervision: combine noisy heuristic labelling functions (keywords, rules, other models) into probabilistic labels.
  • LLM-assisted labelling: let an LLM pre-label, humans review; measure LLM–human agreement first.
  • Synthetic data: generate examples with LLMs or simulators to cover rare cases, then filter hard for quality and diversity. Validate on real data only.
  • Augmentation that respects label invariances ([[cv-basics]]).

Document your datasets

Record source, collection date, licence, consent, preprocessing, known biases and intended use ("datasheets for datasets"). Your future self and your compliance team will need it.

def uncertainty_sample(model, X_pool, k=100):     # active learning by entropy
    p = model.predict_proba(X_pool)
    entropy = -(p * np.log(p + 1e-12)).sum(1)
    return np.argsort(-entropy)[:k]               # indices to send for labelling
