Initialization

For \(z=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{in}}w_jx_j\) with independent zero-mean terms, \(\operatorname{Var}(z)=n_{in}\operatorname{Var}(w)\operatorname{Var}(x)\). To keep activations from growing or shrinking layer by layer we want \(n_{in}\operatorname{Var}(w)\approx1\):

$$\text{Xavier/Glorot (tanh): }\operatorname{Var}(w)=\frac{2}{n_{in}+n_{out}},\qquad \text{He/Kaiming (ReLU): }\operatorname{Var}(w)=\frac{2}{n_{in}}$$

The factor 2 for ReLU compensates for it zeroing half its inputs.

Normalization layers

$$\hat x=\frac{x-\mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\epsilon}},\qquad y=\gamma\hat x+\beta$$
  • BatchNorm: \(\mu,\sigma\) over the batch (per channel). Smooths the loss landscape and allows higher learning rates. Uses running averages at inference. Common in CNNs.
  • LayerNorm: \(\mu,\sigma\) over the features of each example. Independent of batch size; the standard in Transformers.
  • RMSNorm: LayerNorm without mean subtraction, \(y=\gamma\,x/\sqrt{\tfrac1d\sum x_i^2+\epsilon}\). Cheaper; used in most modern LLMs.

Regularization

  • Weight decay (L2 / AdamW).
  • Dropout: during training zero each unit with probability \(p\) and scale survivors by \(1/(1-p)\) ("inverted dropout") so expectations match at test time. It acts like training an ensemble of subnetworks.
  • Data augmentation ([[cv-basics]]), early stopping on validation loss, label smoothing (target \(1-\varepsilon\) for the true class, spreading \(\varepsilon\) over the others).

Stability and speed

  • Gradient clipping: if \(\|\mathbf g\|\gt c\), rescale \(\mathbf g\leftarrow c\,\mathbf g/\|\mathbf g\|\). Standard for RNNs and Transformers.
  • Mixed precision: compute in fp16/bf16, keep master weights in fp32; roughly 2× faster and half the memory.
  • Residual connections ([[cnn]]) and normalization are what make 100+ layer networks trainable.

Debugging recipe that saves days

  1. Check the initial loss: for \(C\) balanced classes it should be about \(\log C\) (2.30 for 10 classes).
  2. Overfit one small batch to near-zero loss. If you can't, there's a bug.
  3. Sweep learning rates on a log scale (1e-5 … 1e-1); the right one is usually just below where loss explodes.
  4. Plot train and validation loss, and gradient norms per layer.
  5. Only then add regularization and scale up.
