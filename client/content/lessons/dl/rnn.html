Vanilla RNN

$$\mathbf h_t=\tanh(\mathbf W_{hh}\mathbf h_{t-1}+\mathbf W_{xh}\mathbf x_t+\mathbf b),\qquad \hat{\mathbf y}_t=\mathbf W_{hy}\mathbf h_t$$

The same weights are used at every step. Training unrolls the network through time and backpropagates (BPTT).

Why gradients vanish or explode

$$\frac{\partial\mathbf h_T}{\partial\mathbf h_t}=\prod_{k=t+1}^{T}\operatorname{diag}\big(1-\mathbf h_k^2\big)\,\mathbf W_{hh}$$

A product of \(T-t\) matrices behaves like \(\mathbf W_{hh}^{T-t}\) ([[eigen-svd]]): if its largest singular value is below 1 the gradient vanishes exponentially, above 1 it explodes. So plain RNNs can't learn long-range dependencies. Clipping handles explosion; gating handles vanishing.

LSTM

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf f_t&=\sigma(\mathbf W_f[\mathbf h_{t-1},\mathbf x_t]+\mathbf b_f)&&\text{forget gate}\\ \mathbf i_t&=\sigma(\mathbf W_i[\mathbf h_{t-1},\mathbf x_t]+\mathbf b_i)&&\text{input gate}\\ \mathbf o_t&=\sigma(\mathbf W_o[\mathbf h_{t-1},\mathbf x_t]+\mathbf b_o)&&\text{output gate}\\ \tilde{\mathbf c}_t&=\tanh(\mathbf W_c[\mathbf h_{t-1},\mathbf x_t]+\mathbf b_c)&&\text{candidate}\\ \mathbf c_t&=\mathbf f_t\odot\mathbf c_{t-1}+\mathbf i_t\odot\tilde{\mathbf c}_t&&\text{cell state}\\ \mathbf h_t&=\mathbf o_t\odot\tanh(\mathbf c_t) \end{aligned}$$

The cell state is updated additively, so \(\partial\mathbf c_t/\partial\mathbf c_{t-1}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf f_t)\). When the forget gate is near 1, information and gradients flow across many steps: the same trick as residual connections. The GRU merges gates into update and reset gates, with fewer parameters and similar performance.

Sequence-to-sequence and the bottleneck

For translation, an encoder RNN compresses the source into one vector and a decoder RNN generates the target from it. Squeezing a whole sentence into one fixed vector is a bottleneck. Attention (Bahdanau, 2014) let the decoder look back at all encoder states. The Transformer then kept attention and dropped recurrence entirely.

lstm = nn.LSTM(input_size=32, hidden_size=128, num_layers=2, batch_first=True, dropout=0.1)
x = torch.randn(16, 50, 32)            # (batch, time, features)
out, (h_n, c_n) = lstm(x)              # out: (16, 50, 128)
