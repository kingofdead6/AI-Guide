Structure of a series

A series usually mixes trend, seasonality (daily, weekly, yearly cycles), events (holidays, promotions) and noise. A series is (weakly) stationary if its mean and autocovariance don't change over time; differencing \(y'_t=y_t-y_{t-1}\) often makes it so. The autocorrelation function \(\rho_k=\operatorname{Corr}(y_t,y_{t-k})\) reveals seasonality and memory.

Classical models

$$\text{AR}(p):\ y_t=c+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iy_{t-i}+\varepsilon_t,\qquad \text{MA}(q):\ y_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}$$

ARIMA(p, d, q) combines them after differencing \(d\) times; SARIMA adds seasonal terms. Exponential smoothing (\(\hat y_{t+1}=\alpha y_t+(1-\alpha)\hat y_t\), extended by Holt–Winters to trend and season) is a strong, cheap baseline.

The ML approach

Turn forecasting into supervised learning: features = lags (\(y_{t-1},y_{t-7},y_{t-28}\)), rolling means and standard deviations, calendar features (day of week, holiday), and known-future covariates (price, planned promotions). Train gradient-boosted trees as a global model across many related series. Deep models (N-BEATS, DeepAR, Temporal Fusion Transformer) and pretrained time-series foundation models also exist; always compare them to seasonal-naive and GBDT baselines.

Evaluation without leakage

  • Backtesting: train on data up to time \(t\), forecast the next \(h\) steps, slide \(t\) forward and repeat (expanding or rolling window). Never shuffle.
  • Metrics: MAE, RMSE, MASE (error relative to a seasonal-naive forecast; scale-free), MAPE (undefined near zero, penalizes over-forecasts more).
  • Uncertainty: predict quantiles with the pinball loss \(L_\tau(y,\hat y)=\max\big(\tau(y-\hat y),(\tau-1)(y-\hat y)\big)\); \(\tau=0.5\) gives the median.
import pandas as pd, numpy as np
from sklearn.ensemble import HistGradientBoostingRegressor
from sklearn.model_selection import TimeSeriesSplit
from sklearn.metrics import mean_absolute_error

df = pd.read_csv("sales.csv", parse_dates=["date"]).set_index("date")   # column: y
for lag in [1, 7, 14, 28]:
    df[f"lag_{lag}"] = df["y"].shift(lag)
df["roll_mean_7"] = df["y"].shift(1).rolling(7).mean()     # shift first: no peeking at today
df["dow"] = df.index.dayofweek; df["month"] = df.index.month
df = df.dropna()
X, y = df.drop(columns="y"), df["y"]

for tr, te in TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=5, test_size=28).split(X):
    m = HistGradientBoostingRegressor(max_iter=500, learning_rate=0.05).fit(X.iloc[tr], y.iloc[tr])
    naive = y.shift(7).iloc[te]                               # seasonal-naive baseline
    print(mean_absolute_error(y.iloc[te], m.predict(X.iloc[te])), mean_absolute_error(y.iloc[te], naive))
