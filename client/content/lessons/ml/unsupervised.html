k-Means

$$\min_{\{\boldsymbol\mu_k\},\{c_i\}}\sum_{i=1}^n\|\mathbf x_i-\boldsymbol\mu_{c_i}\|^2$$

Lloyd's algorithm alternates: assign each point to its nearest centroid, then move each centroid to the mean of its points. Each step can only decrease the objective, so it converges, but to a local optimum. Use k-means++ initialization and several restarts; pick \(k\) with the elbow method or silhouette score.

Gaussian mixtures and EM

$$p(\mathbf x)=\sum_{k=1}^K\pi_k\,\mathcal N(\mathbf x\mid\boldsymbol\mu_k,\boldsymbol\Sigma_k)$$

E-step: responsibilities \(\gamma_{ik}=\dfrac{\pi_k\mathcal N(\mathbf x_i\mid\boldsymbol\mu_k,\boldsymbol\Sigma_k)}{\sum_j\pi_j\mathcal N(\mathbf x_i\mid\boldsymbol\mu_j,\boldsymbol\Sigma_j)}\) (soft assignments). M-step: with \(N_k=\sum_i\gamma_{ik}\), update \(\boldsymbol\mu_k=\frac1{N_k}\sum_i\gamma_{ik}\mathbf x_i\), \(\boldsymbol\Sigma_k=\frac1{N_k}\sum_i\gamma_{ik}(\mathbf x_i-\boldsymbol\mu_k)(\mathbf x_i-\boldsymbol\mu_k)^\top\), \(\pi_k=N_k/n\). EM increases the likelihood monotonically by maximizing a lower bound, the same ELBO that appears in VAEs. k-means is the hard-assignment, equal-spherical-covariance limit of this.

PCA, derived

Find the unit direction \(\mathbf u\) along which centered data has maximum variance:

$$\max_{\mathbf u}\ \mathbf u^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf u\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf u^\top\mathbf u=1\;\xrightarrow{\text{Lagrangian}}\;\nabla_{\mathbf u}\big[\mathbf u^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf u-\lambda(\mathbf u^\top\mathbf u-1)\big]=0\;\Rightarrow\;\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf u=\lambda\mathbf u$$

So \(\mathbf u\) is an eigenvector, and the variance captured is \(\mathbf u^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf u=\lambda\): take the top eigenvector. Subsequent components are the next eigenvectors. In practice compute the SVD of centered \(\mathbf X\): the right singular vectors are the principal directions and \(\lambda_i=\sigma_i^2/(n-1)\). The explained variance ratio of component \(i\) is \(\lambda_i/\sum_j\lambda_j\).

def pca(X, k):
    Xc = X - X.mean(0)
    U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(Xc, full_matrices=False)
    explained = S**2 / (S**2).sum()
    return Xc @ Vt[:k].T, Vt[:k], explained[:k]
