Model and loss

$$\hat{\mathbf y}=\mathbf X\mathbf w,\qquad \mathcal L(\mathbf w)=\|\mathbf y-\mathbf X\mathbf w\|_2^2$$

(Absorb the bias by adding a column of ones to \(\mathbf X\).)

Derivation: the normal equations

$$\nabla_{\mathbf w}\mathcal L=-2\mathbf X^\top(\mathbf y-\mathbf X\mathbf w)=0\;\Rightarrow\;\mathbf X^\top\mathbf X\mathbf w=\mathbf X^\top\mathbf y\;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{\hat{\mathbf w}=(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf X)^{-1}\mathbf X^\top\mathbf y}$$

Geometry: \(\mathbf X\hat{\mathbf w}\) is the orthogonal projection of \(\mathbf y\) onto the column space of \(\mathbf X\); the residual is perpendicular to every feature column (\(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf r=0\)). Probability: with \(y=\mathbf w^\top\mathbf x+\varepsilon,\ \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\), this is the MLE ([[estimation]]).

Regularization

$$\text{Ridge: }\min_{\mathbf w}\|\mathbf y-\mathbf X\mathbf w\|^2+\lambda\|\mathbf w\|_2^2\;\Rightarrow\;\hat{\mathbf w}=(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf X+\lambda\mathbf I)^{-1}\mathbf X^\top\mathbf y$$

Adding \(\lambda\mathbf I\) makes the matrix always invertible and shrinks weights, which reduces variance. Lasso uses \(\lambda\|\mathbf w\|_1\): its diamond-shaped constraint region has corners on the axes, so solutions are often exactly zero, giving automatic feature selection. It has no closed form (use coordinate descent). Elastic net combines both.

Nonlinear features: linear regression is linear in \(\mathbf w\), not in \(\mathbf x\). Add \(x^2\), \(x_1x_2\), \(\log x\), etc., and it fits curves.

# From scratch: closed form and gradient descent
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
X = np.c_[np.ones(200), rng.normal(size=(200, 2))]
w_true = np.array([1.0, 2.0, -3.0]); y = X @ w_true + 0.5 * rng.normal(size=200)

w_closed = np.linalg.solve(X.T @ X, X.T @ y)            # never invert explicitly
w = np.zeros(3)
for _ in range(500):
    w -= 0.1 * (2 / len(y)) * X.T @ (X @ w - y)          # gradient of the mean squared error
print(w_closed, w)

from sklearn.linear_model import Ridge, Lasso
print(Lasso(alpha=0.1).fit(X[:, 1:], y).coef_)
Practice problems
  1. Prove the residual \(\mathbf r=\mathbf y-\mathbf X\hat{\mathbf w}\) is orthogonal to every column of \(\mathbf X\).
    Solution
    The normal equations say \(\mathbf X^\top(\mathbf y-\mathbf X\hat{\mathbf w})=\mathbf 0\), i.e. \(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf r=\mathbf0\).
  2. Derive ridge's closed form.
    Solution
    \(\nabla=-2\mathbf X^\top(\mathbf y-\mathbf X\mathbf w)+2\lambda\mathbf w=0\Rightarrow(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf X+\lambda\mathbf I)\mathbf w=\mathbf X^\top\mathbf y\).
