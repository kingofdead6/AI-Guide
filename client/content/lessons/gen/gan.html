$$\min_G\max_D\;V(D,G)=\mathbb E_{\mathbf x\sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(\mathbf x)]+\mathbb E_{\mathbf z\sim p(\mathbf z)}[\log(1-D(G(\mathbf z)))]$$

What the game optimizes

For a fixed \(G\), maximizing \(p_{\text{data}}\log D+p_g\log(1-D)\) pointwise gives the optimal discriminator

$$D^*(\mathbf x)=\frac{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf x)}{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf x)+p_g(\mathbf x)},\qquad V(D^*,G)=-\log4+2\cdot\mathrm{JSD}(p_{\text{data}}\,\|\,p_g)$$

So the generator minimizes the Jensen–Shannon divergence, which is minimized exactly when \(p_g=p_{\text{data}}\).

In practice

  • Non-saturating loss: train \(G\) to maximize \(\log D(G(\mathbf z))\) rather than minimize \(\log(1-D(G(\mathbf z)))\), which has vanishing gradients early when \(D\) easily wins.
  • Failure modes: mode collapse (the generator produces few variants), oscillation, discriminator overpowering. Remedies include Wasserstein GANs with gradient penalty, spectral normalization, and careful architecture (StyleGAN).
  • GANs give sharp, fast samples but are hard to train. Diffusion models have largely replaced them for image generation; GAN-style adversarial losses still appear inside other systems (e.g. some VAE decoders and audio vocoders).
